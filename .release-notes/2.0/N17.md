## Changed

- When a `docx-editor`, `xlsx-editor` or `visio-viewer` is removed from the page and not attached again before the current task ends, it adds one constructed stylesheet of its own to `document.adoptedStyleSheets` (on first use) and changes its rule. The rule matches nothing. A host that replaces `document.adoptedStyleSheets` may drop it; the next removal adds it again (`ooxml-ui`).

## Fixed

- In Chrome and Edge, a closed `docx-editor`, `xlsx-editor` or `visio-viewer` is freed again when its document drew a picture, shape, form control, chart or effect that is clipped, masked, filtered or filled through a `url(#id)` reference, such as a tiled or cropped Word picture, an Excel shape or drop-down with text, or a Visio glow. The browser's style cache kept such an editor, with its whole document, alive until the page's styles next changed. Removing the editor now changes the page's styles, which empties that cache (`ooxml-ui`, the `docx-*`, `xlsx-*` and `visio-*` viewer packages, and the Office suite).

## Performance

- Office suite, Chromium, three open-and-close cycles per document, three runs each: a closed Word document with a tiled picture stayed in memory after 6 of 9 closes (about 3.3 MB of detached page each) and now after none. A closed Excel workbook with shapes and form controls stayed after 2 of 9 closes (about 1.6 MB each) and now after none. PowerPoint documents were already freed, since the PowerPoint viewer draws its slides outside a shadow root.
